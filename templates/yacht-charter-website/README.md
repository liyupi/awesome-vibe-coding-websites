A two-page site for a crewed yacht charter house, dressed like a chart table: deep navy ground, white panels, hairline brass, and deck-gray mono labels for everything measurable. The first screen is a yacht at anchor in brilliant Mediterranean light — white hull, deep azure water — under a crisp sans headline and a mono label naming the waters. The fleet grid is the working deck: four yachts on white cards with mono spec lines (meters, guests, cabins, type), filtered by type, guest count and cabin count, and the grid visibly re-sorts with a layout animation when a filter lands. Each card links to a query-routed detail page that opens on the flagship when entered bare: photo strip, a mono specification table, and a deck plan drawn in SVG — hull, cabins, salon, cockpit and swim platform labeled with brass leader lines — plus the sample weekly rate and a jump to the quote form. Back on the home page, two sample itineraries plot on an illustrated route map: invented anchorages, a dotted brass route, numbered stops that swap when you toggle seven nights against a long weekend. The quote request — yacht, season, guests, notes — saves locally with an honest note that nothing is sent. Photography stays in one sea-light grade: bright midday clarity, salt haze, yachts, teak and coastline, crew only distant. Swap brand, waters, port and entry rate in one content file; the second demo case turns the same house into a day-charter catamaran fleet.

## Make it yours

- A crewed sailing fleet in the Aegean: set BRAND and PORT to your base, replace the four fleet entries with your sailing yachts, and rewrite both itineraries with your real island stops.
- A day-charter catamaran operator: keep the filterable fleet grid, drop the weekly rate in favor of a per-day entry rate, and shorten the route map to a single afternoon loop.
- A charter broker with no boats of its own: use the fleet cards for your represented yachts, point the quote form at your enquiry inbox, and label every rate as sample until confirmed.
- A boutique brokerage studio building for a client: set BRAND, WATERS and PORT per client, swap the sea-light photo strip for their own yacht photography, and keep the mono spec lines for measured detail.

## FAQ

### How do I change the yacht fleet, rates and cruising waters in the Yacht Charter Website template?
The Yacht Charter Website template keeps every user-facing value in one content file: the brand, waters, port, entry weekly rate and the fleet array with each yacht's name, type, meters, guests, cabins, rate, photo and blurb. Edit those entries and both the home page and the yacht detail page update. The itineraries with their stops and route paths live in the same file, so routes and rates stay consistent across the site.

### How does the fleet filter and deck plan work on the Yacht Charter Website template?
The Yacht Charter Website template filters its four-yacht fleet grid by three groups: type (sailing or motor), guest count (up to 8 or 9 to 12) and cabin count (4+ or 6+). Matching cards re-sort with a layout animation, and non-matching cards fade out. Each card opens a query-routed detail page with a photo strip, a mono specification table and an SVG deck plan that labels hull, cabins, salon, cockpit and swim platform with brass leader lines.

### Can I use the Yacht Charter Website template for a client charter brokerage?
Commercial and client work with the Yacht Charter Website template requires Pro. Pro templates show the brief, stack and variables publicly and give Pro members the full prompt, and the source code of the demos comes with Pro. Free prompts and every DESIGN.md are licensed for personal, non-commercial use only. A brokerage can set the brand, port, waters and fleet, then hand the built site to the client.

### Does the quote request form on the Yacht Charter Website template actually send enquiries?
The Yacht Charter Website template saves the quote request locally and labels it honestly: nothing is sent. The form collects yacht, season, guests and notes, and you reach it from the yacht detail page or the home page quote section. To take real enquiries you would connect the form to your own mailing list or booking system; the template itself has no backend, server code or external APIs.

### What should I change first when adapting the Yacht Charter Website template to my own charter house?
Start with the four variables: set BRAND to your charter house, WATERS to your cruising ground, PORT to your home port and WEEK_FROM to your entry weekly rate. Then replace the sample fleet entries and the two sample itineraries, including stop names and route positions, and rewrite the hero and quote copy so the words match your waters. The navy, brass and mono styling stays as described.
