# Constellation Story Map

Build this website exactly as described below. Replace the [VARIABLES] with your own values; keep everything else.

## Brief
Build [BRAND], an original single-page celestial story atlas presenting [COLLECTION_NAME] with the headline [HEADLINE]. The default guide contains the six comma-separated constellations in [CONSTELLATION_SET], with original retellings shaped around [STORY_LENS] for [AUDIENCE]. Visitors drag and zoom a code-drawn star field, select constellations to trace their lines and read their stories, shift an explicitly illustrative sky by date, and connect maker stars into named creations saved locally. Use only procedural Canvas 2D, local SVG, and original copy; this is not a reproduction of an existing planetarium or star atlas.

- Audience: Write the introduction, story summaries, and help text for [AUDIENCE]. A first-time visitor should understand the controls without prior astronomy knowledge. Keep the myths accessible without turning them into horoscopes, and make every canvas action available through ordinary DOM controls for keyboard and touch users.
- Goal: Within five seconds, let a visitor see a richly detailed sky, one traced constellation, and a readable story. Make dragging, selection, date changes, and the create-save loop feel immediate and reliable. Preserve selected stories and saved creations across rerenders, keep saved creations across reloads, and deliver the complete experience as a static site with no external data request.
- Tone: Curious, lyrical, precise, and gently editorial. Treat classical myth as cultural storytelling rather than scientific fact or personal prediction. Use concise US English, concrete interaction feedback, and original modern retellings. Clearly call the star geometry and date behavior illustrative; never imply location-aware observing guidance, live ephemeris data, astrology, or fortune-telling.

## Variables
- [BRAND] — Atlas brand (default: "Asterwyn"). An invented short name for the wordmark, document title, local-storage namespace, and guide; never a real brand.
- [COLLECTION_NAME] — Sky collection name (default: "The Lantern Sky"). A short invented title for the current collection of six constellation stories.
- [HEADLINE] — Welcome headline (default: "Find the stories between the stars."). A clear five-to-ten-word invitation; adapt supporting copy if its meaning changes.
- [AUDIENCE] — Audience (default: "curious stargazers and families reading together"). A plain-language phrase used to adapt the welcome, stories, and help copy.
- [CONSTELLATION_SET] — Six constellation names (default: "Lyra, Cygnus, Cassiopeia, Orion, Ursa Major, Corona Borealis"). Exactly six comma-separated public-domain constellation names in the order of the six supplied geometry records; write original stories for each.
- [STORY_LENS] — Story themes (default: "wonder, courage, and connection"). Three broad themes used to shape the original retellings without prophecy or advice.
- [CREDIT] — keep a small "Template by VibeFather" link to https://vibefather.ai/t/constellation-story-map in the footer (default: yes; delete this line to remove it)

The copy under Pages & Sections is written for the default values. If your values differ, rewrite every headline, paragraph, label and image for them — same length, tone and structure. Layout, colors, type and motion stay as described.
Keep all copy, image paths and links in one content file (for example `src/content.ts`), keyed by section, so the content can be swapped later without touching components.

## Don'ts
- Do not copy, trace, recolor, or closely imitate an existing planetarium, star atlas, educational website, or astronomy product, including its code, prompt text, map projection, interface composition, illustrations, icons, copy, slogans, names, logos, or mascot. The procedural layout and visual treatment here must remain original.
- Do not use third-party star-atlas artwork, photographs, sky textures, constellation illustrations, downloaded map tiles, proprietary zodiac art, licensed characters, real people's likenesses, or any brand logo. Use only the declared original Canvas and SVG geometry and local utility icons.
- Do not copy mythology passages, guidebook entries, educational copy, or story-panel text from another source. Classical constellation names and broad public-domain myths may inform the six records, but every retelling, epithet, note, and instruction must be newly written.
- Do not present the toy as a live astronomical catalog, location-aware sky forecast, scientific ephemeris, astrology reading, horoscope, or source of personal predictions. Keep the illustrative-sky disclosure visible and do not invent precise coordinates, visibility claims, or observing conditions.
- No backend, server code, API routes, database, authentication, astronomy API, geolocation request, remote form submission, analytics, third-party API, runtime fetch, tracking pixel, social widget, or account. Saved creations stay in validated localStorage and contain only names and maker-star ids.
- No CDN scripts or styles, Google Fonts URLs, hotlinked assets, externally hosted icons, placeholder-image services, Unsplash, Pexels, Pixabay, Google Images, or assets with unclear, noncommercial, or share-alike licenses. Use Vite base './', relative paths, locally bundled packages, and self-hosted Fontsource fonts.
- Do not add image generation, Three.js, WebGL, a canvas helper library, a state-management library, an animation framework, a router, or external astronomy data. Native Canvas 2D, SVG, CSS, React state, and the declared packages are sufficient.
- Do not hard-code default brand, collection, headline, audience, constellation set, story themes, story text, or storage namespace outside the content/data modules. Alternate values must produce six coherent new stories and interface copy with no default remnants or unresolved [VARIABLE] placeholders.
- Do not confuse taps with drags, attach touch-action none to the whole page, hide creation behind pointer-only input, or let a date change erase the selected story, view, or saved collection. Follow movement thresholds, hit areas, state rules, and keyboard-equivalent controls exactly.
- Do not run unbounded animation, per-frame React rendering, per-frame star-array allocation, uncapped device pixel ratio, animation while hidden, automatic camera movement, or motion that ignores prefers-reduced-motion. Keep the fixed star counts, frame caps, and cleanup requirements.
- Do not use a splash screen, autoplay audio, scroll-jacking, pinned sections, a custom cursor, excessive bloom, glass-card decoration, fake testimonials, visitor counts, pricing, newsletter forms, or marketing sections. The toy itself is the first screen.
