A portfolio for a director of photography, built the way a reel is cut: the image first and the words underneath. The first screen is a letterboxed frame cycling through graded film stills with a slow push-in, captioned in a mono credit line and set on deep black with tungsten amber and teal shadows. The project list is a credits roll: title, director, format and year, with the still floating beside the pointer as you move down it. Each project page opens on its title card, shows the still with enlarged detail crops, and then does the thing most portfolios skip: a top-down lighting breakdown drawn in code, with camera, key, fill and practicals that you can switch on and off to read the notes for each one. A reel section plays the stills under a running timecode, gear notes list the kit in plain words, and a contact panel covers representation and bookings, with a CV that downloads as a text file. All stills share one grade, people appear from behind or in silhouette, and the type is a wide display face with mono credits. Change the name, city and featured film in the content file and swap in your own stills under the same file names; layout, palette and motion stay as described.

## Make it yours

- A commercial director of photography: set BRAND and CITY, then swap the credits roll for your spot work and rewrite the gear notes around the camera and lighting kit you actually own.
- A music video cinematographer: point FEATURED_FILM at one recent video, replace the stills with graded frames and keep the lighting breakdowns for the setups producers ask about most.
- A film student building a first portfolio: use the credits roll for shorts and student films, trim the gear notes and lead the contact panel with a reel request instead of representation.
- A camera assistant moving into shooting: keep the lighting breakdowns as your strongest proof, list the crews you worked with in the credits and set the CV download to your full credits.

## FAQ

### What does the Cinematographer Portfolio Website template include?
The Cinematographer Portfolio Website template includes a letterboxed hero cycling through graded film stills, a project list laid out as a credits roll, project pages with stills grids and enlarged detail crops, a top-down lighting breakdown with camera, key, fill and practicals you can toggle, a reel section with a running timecode, gear notes, a contact panel for representation and bookings, and a CV that downloads as a text file.

### How does the lighting breakdown work on the Cinematographer Portfolio Website?
The Cinematographer Portfolio Website draws each lighting setup as an inline diagram in code: the set, the camera with its view cone, the key, fill and practicals with their beam cones, and the subject. You switch each light on and off to read the note for it. Setups live in the content file as typed data, so you add a new one by describing the lights rather than drawing anything.

### How do I put my own film stills into the Cinematographer Portfolio Website?
The Cinematographer Portfolio Website keeps every still path in one content file, so you swap in your own images under the same file names and the layout, palette and motion stay as they are. The template is built around one shared grade with tungsten highlights and teal shadows and people shown from behind or in silhouette, so pick stills that sit together in that look.

### What should I change first in the Cinematographer Portfolio Website template?
Start with the three variables: set BRAND to your name, CITY to your base and FEATURED_FILM to the project you want on the first screen. Then rewrite the credits roll with your own titles, directors, formats and years, and replace the default lighting setups and gear notes so the proofs on the page are yours.
