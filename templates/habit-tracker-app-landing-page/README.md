Daybloom is a single-page landing site for a mobile app, written around an invented habit tracker. It is built for people launching a consumer app who want the page itself to feel like the product: soft, friendly and a little playful. The page sits on a warm cream background with drifting peach, lilac, mint and butter gradient blobs, large rounded cards and a violet accent used only for actions. The centerpiece is a phone drawn entirely in CSS, with a frame, camera pill, status bar and home indicator and no device artwork. In the features section the phone stays pinned beside the copy, and its screen cross-fades between four in-app views (today's check-ins, a forgiving streak, a weekly chart and a reminder feed) as each feature scrolls past. On phones the pinned layout turns into a stacked one, with a smaller phone above each step. Below that is a working streak calendar. Visitors pick one of three habits, tap days to mark them done, and watch the current streak, longest streak and completion ring update, with a small burst of confetti dots at milestones. The rest of the page covers the usual launch needs: a slow marquee of habit chips, six testimonial cards with drawn initial avatars instead of photos or company logos, Free and Plus pricing with a sliding monthly and yearly switch, a short FAQ and a closing download card. The download buttons are plain pills drawn in code, not store badges. Everything is code-drawn or comes from Lucide, so no image licenses need tracking. To adapt it, change the brand, what the app does, who it is for, the four example habits and the Plus price. The layout, palette and motion stay the same, so a plant-care app or a study timer fits just as well.

## FAQ

### How does the CSS phone in the Habit Tracker App Landing Page change screens while scrolling?
The Habit Tracker App Landing Page pins a phone drawn entirely in CSS beside the features copy, and its screen cross-fades between four in-app views as each feature scrolls past: today's check-ins, a forgiving streak, a weekly chart and a reminder feed. On mobile the pinned layout stacks, with a smaller phone above each step. When a visitor prefers reduced motion, the screens swap instantly instead of fading.

### Can visitors try the habit tracker before downloading it on this landing page?
Yes. The Habit Tracker App Landing Page includes a working streak calendar. Visitors pick one of three habits, tap days to mark them done, and the current streak, longest streak and completion ring update live, with a small burst of confetti dots at milestones. That gives people a feel for the app before they install anything, and every primary button leads to the download card.

### What do I change to make this app landing page fit a different app?
The Habit Tracker App Landing Page is driven by five variables: BRAND, APP_PURPOSE, AUDIENCE, HABIT_EXAMPLES and PLUS_PRICE. Change the app name, the one-line description of what the app does, who it is for, the four example habits and the paid plan price, then rewrite the headlines, feature copy, testimonials, FAQ and reminders to match. The layout, pastel palette and motion stay as they are.

### Does the Habit Tracker App Landing Page need image licenses or store badges?
No. Everything in the Habit Tracker App Landing Page is drawn in code or comes from Lucide, including the phone frame, camera pill, status bar, testimonial initial avatars and the download pills, which are plain buttons rather than store badges. That means no image licenses to track, and the page stays consistent if you change the brand colors.
