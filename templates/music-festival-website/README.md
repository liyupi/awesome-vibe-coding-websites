# Music Festival Website <sup>PRO</sup>

> A three-page festival site with a sound-reactive hero, filterable line-up, illustrated map and ticket table.

This is a three-page website for a summer music festival, built around a hero that listens to real sound. The first screen shows a wide photograph of a festival field at sunset with a slow generative sun drawn over it. Press play and an original eight-bar loop, generated by a small script in the project, starts through the Web Audio API; the sun and its ring of bars then follow live frequency data. Nothing makes a sound until the visitor asks for it, and pause and mute stay within reach.

The home page continues with a poster-style line-up where type size steps down by billing tier and the names reflow when you filter by day. Hovering a name shows an artist photo, a small live waveform and a short synthesized snippet; on a phone, a tap does the same. A dense three-day schedule follows on a flat yellow field. The map page is an illustrated inline SVG with stages, food, camping, water and first aid, legend filters and a detail panel. The tickets page compares four passes in a table with a sticky header, which becomes swipeable cards on mobile, and adds a price calculator with no checkout.

It suits promoters, venues, city weekends and community music events that want a site with more personality than a ticketing page, and developers who want a working example of frequency analysis and an interactive SVG map. The color is loud on purpose: a yellow-to-red sunset gradient and one electric green against deep violet ink, with tall condensed poster type. To adapt it, change the festival name, dates, location, genres and base price; the acts, map labels and ticket prices are rewritten to match, and the layout, colors and motion stay the same.

## Variables

Replace these in the prompt with your own values; everything else stays as written.

| Variable | What it is | In the demo |
|---|---|---|
| `[BRAND]` | Festival name | Sunspill |
| `[EVENT_DATES]` | Festival dates | July 16–18, 2027 |
| `[LOCATION]` | Site and town | Cinder Meadows, Port Alder, WA |
| `[LINEUP_GENRES]` | Genres on the bill | indie, disco and warm electronics |
| `[GA_PRICE]` | 3-Day General pass price | $189 |

## Make it yours

- A city weekend festival: set BRAND and EVENT_DATES, swap the camping zones on the map for transit stops, and rewrite the line-up for local acts.
- A venue running a summer series: point LOCATION at the venue, replace the three-day schedule with weekly dates, and keep the ticket table for season passes.
- A community music event: set GA_PRICE low, rewrite LINEUP_GENRES for school and folk acts, and use the map for halls, food stalls and first aid.
- A promoter pitching sponsors: keep the line-up and ticket calculator, set BRAND to the event, and add sponsor names to the poster-wall type tiers.

## FAQ

### Is there a music festival website template I can build with AI?

The Music Festival Website template on VibeFather is a three-page festival site built from one published prompt: a sound-reactive hero, a filterable line-up with hover previews, an illustrated SVG site map and a ticket table with a price calculator. ### How does the sound-reactive hero on the Music Festival Website template work?
The hero shows a festival field at sunset with a generative sun drawn over it. A small script in the project generates an original eight-bar loop, played through the Web Audio API; the sun and its ring of bars follow live frequency data from an analyser. Nothing plays until the visitor presses play, and pause and mute stay within reach. The engine also ducks the loop while an artist snippet plays.

### How do I change the festival name, dates and ticket prices in this template?
Edit the five variables: BRAND sets the festival name that fills the hero headline, EVENT_DATES drives the day tabs and schedule labels, LOCATION names the site and map places, LINEUP_GENRES rewrites the invented acts and genre labels, and GA_PRICE sets the General pass price with the other tiers calculated from it. All copy, image paths and links sit in one content file, so a new festival swaps cleanly.

### What can visitors do on the line-up and map pages of the Music Festival Website template?
The home page line-up is poster-style, with type size stepping down by billing tier and names reflowing when filtered by day. Hovering or tapping a name shows an artist photo, a small live waveform and a short synthesized snippet. The map page is an illustrated inline SVG with stages, food, camping, water and first aid, legend filters and a detail panel for each place.
