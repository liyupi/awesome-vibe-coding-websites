# Music Festival Website <sup>PRO</sup>

> A three-page festival site with a sound-reactive hero, filterable line-up, illustrated map and ticket table.

This is a three-page website for a summer music festival, built around a hero that listens to real sound. The first screen shows a wide photograph of a festival field at sunset with a slow generative sun drawn over it. Press play and an original eight-bar loop, generated by a small script in the project, starts through the Web Audio API; the sun and its ring of bars then follow live frequency data. Nothing makes a sound until the visitor asks for it, and pause and mute stay within reach.

The home page continues with a poster-style line-up where type size steps down by billing tier and the names reflow when you filter by day. Hovering a name shows an artist photo, a small live waveform and a short synthesized snippet; on a phone, a tap does the same. A dense three-day schedule follows on a flat yellow field. The map page is an illustrated inline SVG with stages, food, camping, water and first aid, legend filters and a detail panel. The tickets page compares four passes in a table with a sticky header, which becomes swipeable cards on mobile, and adds a price calculator with no checkout.

It suits promoters, venues, city weekends and community music events that want a site with more personality than a ticketing page, and developers who want a working example of frequency analysis and an interactive SVG map. The color is loud on purpose: a yellow-to-red sunset gradient and one electric green against deep violet ink, with tall condensed poster type. To adapt it, change the festival name, dates, location, genres and base price; the acts, map labels and ticket prices are rewritten to match, and the layout, colors and motion stay the same.

## Variables

Replace these in the prompt with your own values; everything else stays as written.

| `[EVENT_DATES]` | Festival dates | July 16–18, 2027 |
| `[LOCATION]` | Site and town | Cinder Meadows, Port Alder, WA |
| `[LINEUP_GENRES]` | Genres on the bill | indie, disco and warm electronics |
| `[GA_PRICE]` | 3-Day General pass price | $189 |

## Make it yours

- A city weekend promoter: set BRAND to the event name, EVENT_DATES to the three days and LOCATION to the downtown blocks, then rewrite the map zones for plazas and transit stops.
- A community music festival: use LINEUP_GENRES for local folk and brass acts, lower GA_PRICE for a family day rate, and relabel the camping zone as a picnic lawn.
- A venue running a summer series: keep the poster line-up and schedule, swap the map stages for rooms and bars, and point the ticket table at your box office.
- A bass and club weekend: set LINEUP_GENRES to the bill, push GA_PRICE up for the Camp Weekend pass, and rename the map zones for late-night stages and water points.

## FAQ

### How does the sound-reactive hero on the Music Festival Website work?
The Music Festival Website template ships an original eight-bar loop generated by a small script in the project, played through the Web Audio API. Nothing makes a sound until the visitor presses play; the sun and its ring of bars then follow live frequency data from an analyser. Pause and mute stay in reach, and if the audio cannot load the visuals keep running.

### Can I change the festival name, dates and ticket prices in the Music Festival Website template?
Yes. The Music Festival Website template is driven by five variables: BRAND sets the festival name, EVENT_DATES sets the three days and their tabs, LOCATION sets the site and town, LINEUP_GENRES rewrites the act names and genre labels, and GA_PRICE sets the General pass price and scales the other tiers from it. All copy lives in one content file so you can swap it without touching the layout.

### Does the Music Festival Website template sell tickets or take payments?
No. The Music Festival Website template compares four passes in a table with a sticky header that becomes swipeable cards on mobile, and adds a price calculator, but there is no checkout. The ticket call to action stays visible at every scroll position on every page, so you can point it at your own ticketing provider and keep the layout as the front door.

### Can I use the Music Festival Website template for a client's event?

The Music Festival Website template is a Pro template: the brief, stack and variables are public, the full prompt is available to Pro members, and the source code of the demos comes with Pro. Free templates' prompts and every DESIGN.md are licensed for personal, non-commercial use only.

### What should I change first in the Music Festival Website template?
Start with BRAND and EVENT_DATES, because the hero headline is sized to fit the festival name and the day tabs and schedule labels derive from the dates. Then set LOCATION and LINEUP_GENRES so the map labels and invented acts match, and set GA_PRICE to your General pass so the other three tiers scale from it. The colors, poster type and motion stay as they are.
