# Sushi Omakase Restaurant Website

> A dark, quiet-luxury omakase counter site with scroll-served courses and a seat-picker diagram.

A single-page site for a high-end sushi counter, built around one idea: luxury that stays quiet. The page opens on a full-bleed macro photograph of nigiri on dark lacquered cedar, lit by a single warm lamp, with the headline set into the shadow. Below it come the house philosophy, the season's eight courses, the chef's story, an interactive counter-seat reservation, and the practical details of finding the door. The seasonal menu is the centerpiece: each course reveals itself as you scroll, one dish at a time, while a thin progress rail fills amber as the meal moves forward. The reservation section replaces a booking form with a diagram of the eight-seat counter: pick a date from the next seven days, choose the 17:30 or 20:00 seating, and click a seat; a quiet inline confirmation closes the loop. Everything ships as static files with no backend, so the picker is pure interface. Design-wise the template commits to lacquer black, charred surfaces, cedar hairlines and one muted amber accent, with Cormorant Garamond display type over Karla text. Motion is deliberately slow: smooth scrolling, gentle parallax on the photography, and 750ms reveals that honor prefers-reduced-motion. There are no white cards, no pill buttons and no gradients; structure comes from hairlines, whitespace and type on the lacquer ground. It suits any tasting-counter business: sushi, kaiseki, tempura, a wine or whisky flight bar, a cupping room. Swap brand, chef, price and seat count in one content file, replace the six photographs with your own shoot, and the design holds; the second demo case rebuilds the same layout as a coffee cupping counter to prove it.

## Variables

Replace these in the prompt with your own values; everything else stays as written.

| Variable | What it is | In the demo |
|---|---|---|
| `[BRAND]` | Restaurant name | Kurosumi |
| `[CONCEPT]` | Menu concept | seasonal omakase |
| `[PRICE]` | Tasting price | $185 per guest |
| `[SEATS]` | Counter seats | 8 |
| `[CHEF]` | Chef name | Ren Ishida |

## How to use it

1. Copy [prompt.md](prompt.md), or run `npx vibefather add sushi-omakase-restaurant-website` in your project.
2. Replace the `[VARIABLES]` with your own brand, copy and details.
3. Paste it into Claude Code, Codex, Cursor or another AI coding agent and let it build. [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md) holds the colors, type and spacing on their own if you only want the look.

## Make it yours

- A kaiseki or tempura counter: set [BRAND] and [CHEF] to your house, rewrite the eight courses as your own sequence, and keep the seat diagram with your real [SEATS] count.
- A whisky or wine flight bar: change [CONCEPT] to your tasting flight, replace the nigiri photographs with pours on dark wood, and adjust [PRICE] for your flight.
- A coffee roaster's cupping room: follow the second demo, set [CONCEPT] to a guided cupping flight, and swap the courses for the day's lots and origins.
- A supper club with one shared table: set [SEATS] to your table size, relabel the two seatings as arrival times, and point the confirmation panel at your inbox.

## FAQ

### How does the seat-picker reservation work on the Sushi Omakase Restaurant Website?
The reservation section replaces a booking form with a diagram of the counter. A visitor picks a date from the next seven days, chooses the 17:30 or 20:00 seating, and clicks a seat token; a quiet inline confirmation panel fades in. The picker is pure interface state with no backend, so you would connect it to your own booking system or inbox to receive real requests.

### How do I change the number of courses and the price on the omakase site?
The Sushi Omakase Restaurant Website template keeps every user-facing string in one content file, and the price comes from the [PRICE] variable, shown in the hero sub-line, under the course list and in the reservation section. The default menu is eight courses that reveal one at a time on scroll; edit the course entries in that content file to change names, descriptions or their number.

### Can I reuse this dark counter layout for a non-sushi tasting business?
Yes. The second live demo rebuilds the same layout as a coffee cupping room, which shows the design holds beyond sushi. Set [BRAND], [CONCEPT], [PRICE], [SEATS] and [CHEF] in the content file, then replace the six photographs with your own shoot in the same lighting style: dark lacquered surface, one warm side light, shallow depth of field.

### What should I change first after adding the Sushi Omakase Restaurant Website template?
Start with the five variables: [BRAND] for the wordmark and email, [CONCEPT] for how the tasting is described, [PRICE], [SEATS] for the seat diagram tokens and spelled-out count, and [CHEF] for the chef story. Then swap the six photographs, and rewrite the hero headline and course copy so they match your own menu rather than the default eight-course omakase.
