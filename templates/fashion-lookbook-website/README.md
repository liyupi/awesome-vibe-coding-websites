> A monochrome fashion lookbook with vertical-scroll horizontal chapters and spec-card look overlays.

A lookbook for an invented fashion label, built like a printed issue: near-monochrome photography, one collection accent, and type so big it behaves like architecture. The cover is a full-bleed silhouette — a single model under a sculptural coat, face turned away — with the collection name set across it in oversized condensed type. Then the signature move: the book reads sideways. Vertical scroll drives a pinned viewport whose chapters slide horizontally — chapter titles drifting slower than the photographs, frames carrying their own inner parallax, a thin accent line tracking progress. Two chapters hold five looks; a campaign film appears only as an honest drawn placeholder, poster frame and play glyph, no real video. Clicking any look opens a full-screen detail: the photograph beside a code-drawn spec card of garment credits — fabric, cut, finishing — marked as invented sample copy. A quiet typographic interlude carries the collection notes, and a stockists list closes with invented stores in real cities. Ink ground, bone type, one accent color that is itself a variable; Oswald display over Manrope text; zero radius, hairline structure. Under prefers-reduced-motion the chapters stack vertically and everything still reads. Every photograph comes from one campaign shoot in one hard light: models turned away, shadowed or cropped above the shoulders, silhouettes over portraits, and no readable text anywhere in frame. Swap label, collection, season and accent in one content file; the second demo case turns the issue into a tailoring capsule in deep green.

## Make it yours

- An independent tailoring label: set BRAND and COLLECTION to your capsule, switch ACCENT to deep green and rewrite the garment credits as fabric, cut and finishing for your suits.
- A campaign photographer's portfolio: keep the chapter structure, replace the invented looks with your own shoot frames and turn the stockists list into a list of publications or clients.
- A fashion student presenting a graduate collection: set SEASON to your show term, use the collection notes interlude for your manifesto and keep the drawn film slot as a placeholder.
- A concept store launching a seasonal edit: set BRAND to the store, use the chapters for curated looks and turn the stockists list into stockists of the featured labels.

## FAQ

### How does the horizontal scrolling work on the Fashion Lookbook Website, and can I turn it off?
On the Fashion Lookbook Website, vertical scroll drives a pinned viewport whose chapters slide sideways: the track moves as you scroll, chapter titles drift slower than the photographs, and each frame carries its own inner parallax. The template already handles reduced-motion preferences by stacking the chapters vertically with no pinning, so visitors who ask their device for less motion still read the whole issue.

### What do I change first in the Fashion Lookbook Website to make it my own label?
Start with the four variables in the content file: set BRAND to your label name, COLLECTION to your collection title, SEASON to your season label and ACCENT to your one accent color. Changing ACCENT re-inks the chapter numbers, progress line, play glyph, links and look tags at once. Then replace the sample looks, garment credits, collection notes and stockists with your own.

### Can I use the Fashion Lookbook Website for a client or a commercial fashion label?

Free prompts and every DESIGN.md are licensed for personal, non-commercial use only.

### Does the Fashion Lookbook Website include a real campaign video or real product photos?
No. The Fashion Lookbook Website ships with a drawn campaign-film placeholder, a poster frame with a play glyph and a caption saying no video ships with the demo. The photographs are sample imagery from one campaign shoot, and the garment credits and stockists are marked as invented sample copy, so you replace them with your own shoot and real stockists.

### How do the look-detail overlays and garment credits work on the Fashion Lookbook Website?
Clicking any look on the Fashion Lookbook Website opens a full-screen overlay: the photograph at 3:4 on the left and a code-drawn spec card on the right listing fabric, cut and finishing, with an accent look number. Close it with the X, the Escape key or the backdrop. The credits live in the chapters data in the content file, so you edit garment details there.
