A two-page website for an independent salon and barbershop: a home page with services, prices and durations, stylist profiles, a work gallery and reviews, and a dedicated booking page with a four-step appointment flow. The default content follows an invented six-chair shop in Chicago with four stylists, but the structure suits any salon, barbershop or grooming studio: change the brand, city, street and founding year and every section still holds. The home page opens on a full-bleed photograph of the room — deep green walls, brass-framed mirrors, leather chairs — then a price list grouped into cuts, color, beard and treatments with durations, four stylist cards each with their chair, specialties and working days, a gallery of finished work shot from behind and tool still lifes (no faces anywhere), three customer reviews and a marquee of short pull quotes. The booking page walks through service, stylist, day and time slot, and name, with client-side validation, then shows a confirmation card with a reference number, saved in the browser — no backend, no accounts, nothing submitted. The palette is forest green, warm cream and brass; Bodoni Moda sets the headlines in high-contrast fashion serif and Karla the body, both self-hosted. Closed weekdays disable their dates in the picker, and each stylist's working days filter the calendar. To adapt it, swap the variables and rewrite the copy in one content file; services, stylists, hours and slots all read from it.

## Make it yours

- A single-chair barber working alone: set BRAND to your name, cut the stylist cards to one, and list your own services, prices and working days in the content file.
- A color-focused salon: keep the four-step booking flow, rewrite the service groups around balayage and highlights, and update durations so time slots match longer appointments.
- A neighborhood shop in another city: set CITY, STREET and FOUNDED, then rewrite the hero eyebrow, story line and footer address so every mention matches the new location.
- A grooming studio for walk-ins: keep the open-now indicator and hours block, adjust the service list to beard and shave work, and point clients to the booking page for chair times.

## FAQ

### How does the booking flow on the Hair Salon and Barbershop Website template work?
The Hair Salon and Barbershop Website template walks clients through four steps: service, stylist, day and time slot, then name. Each stylist's working days filter the calendar and closed weekdays disable their dates. The flow validates on the client and ends in a confirmation card with a reference number, saved in the browser. Nothing is submitted to a server and no accounts are created.

### Can I change the stylists, services and prices in the Hair Salon and Barbershop Website template?
Yes. The Hair Salon and Barbershop Website template reads every service, price, duration, stylist, working day, opening hour and review from one content file. Rewrite that file to match your shop and the home page, booking calendar and time slots update together. The four variables CITY, BRAND, STREET and FOUNDED cover the shop name, address and founding year used across both pages.

### Does the Hair Salon and Barbershop Website template show whether the shop is open right now?
The Hair Salon and Barbershop Website template includes an open-now indicator driven by the opening hours table and the visitor's clock. The home page also lists opening hours, and the booking page uses the same hours to build the next fourteen bookable days. Change the hours in the content file and both the indicator and the calendar follow.

### What photography does the Hair Salon and Barbershop Website template need?
The Hair Salon and Barbershop Website template is built around full-bleed photographs of the room and the tools, never faces. The home page opens on a photo of the shop, and the gallery shows finished work shot from behind plus tool still lifes. Supply your own images in the same slots and keep that no-faces rule if you want the same editorial tone.
