> A golden-hour safari lodge site with suites gallery, day timeline and seasonal rates.

A single-page site for a high-end safari lodge, built like a film: charcoal ground, ochre and olive accents, warm grain over everything, and full-bleed landscape photography shot at first and last light. The first screen is a golden-hour savanna with the lodge glowing on a river bend, a refined serif headline over the haze, and a stay price set quietly beneath the buttons. From there the page tells one stay, end to end: an editorial strip on how the lodge behaves as a guest in its own conservancy; a suites gallery of canvas tents at dawn, dusk and lantern light; a day-at-the-lodge timeline that walks down the page — dawn drive, brunch on the deck, siesta, sundowners, night drive — with a rail that fills like the sun crossing the sky. Experience cards cover drives, walking safaris, birding mornings and a community visit, priced as sample data and honest about promising light rather than sightings. A seasonal rates table compares green, shoulder and dry season per suite, drawn in code with hairlines instead of card chrome, and the inquiry form collects season, nights, party size and a note, saving to local storage only and saying so. Fraunces carries the display voice over Work Sans; motion is slow and restrained — hero parallax, 700ms reveals, the filling timeline rail — all honoring prefers-reduced-motion. Swap lodge, conservancy, suite count and starting rate in one content file; the second demo case turns the same layout into a photography-first safari lodge to prove the design holds.

- A photo-safari camp: keep the golden-hour hero and day timeline, replace the experience cards with photography hides and golden-hour drives, and price them in the rates table.

- A wilderness retreat studio pitch: hand the four variables and the content file to a client, then swap the suites gallery and day timeline for their own property before launch.

## FAQ

### How do I change the lodge name, conservancy and nightly rate in the Safari Lodge Website template?
The Safari Lodge Website template keeps every user-facing string in one content file, so you set BRAND to your lodge name, REGION to your conservancy, SUITES to your suite count and RATE_FROM to your starting nightly rate there. The wordmark, page title, hero sub-line, day-at-the-lodge heading, rates table and contact email all read from those values, so nothing needs editing in the layout.

### What does the inquiry form on the Safari Lodge Website template collect, and where do submissions go?
The inquiry form on the Safari Lodge Website template collects season, number of nights, party size and a free-text note. Submissions save to local storage in the visitor's own browser only, and the form says so on the page. There is no backend, server code or external API, so connecting the form to your own inbox or booking system is the change you make before going live.

### How does the seasonal rates table work on the Safari Lodge Website template?
The Safari Lodge Website template draws a seasonal rates table in code with hairlines rather than card chrome, comparing green, shoulder and dry season per suite. The dry season column uses your RATE_FROM starting rate. All rates are sample data in the content file, so replace them with your real per-suite seasonal prices and keep the three-season structure that lets guests compare before they inquire.

### Can I use the Safari Lodge Website template for a photo safari camp or a tented camp rather than a lodge?
Yes. The Safari Lodge Website template was built for safari lodges, tented camps, wilderness retreats and photo-safari outfits, and one of the two live demos is a photography-first safari lodge that keeps the same layout. You change the suites gallery, experience cards and day timeline copy to match your camp, and the golden-hour identity, seasonal rates table and inquiry form carry over unchanged.

### What can I edit first to make the Safari Lodge Website template feel like my own lodge?
Start with the four variables: set BRAND, REGION, SUITES and RATE_FROM, then swap the full-bleed landscape photography for your own first-and-last-light shots and rewrite the conservancy strip so it describes how your lodge behaves as a guest in its landscape. After that, adjust the five day timeline stops, the four experience cards and the seasonal rates to your real offering.

Copy the full prompt (Pro) from this page or run npx vibefather add safari-lodge-website, replace [BRAND], [REGION], [SUITES] and [RATE_FROM] with your own content, and paste it into Claude Code, Codex, Cursor or another AI coding agent. The live demos were built from exactly that text.
