A single-page launch site for one indie game, painted rather than built. The first screen is full-bleed hand-painted key art — a silhouetted traveler with a lantern over a dusk valley — with the game's title set into the painting like a masthead, a quiet ember light drifting across the scene. Scrolling walks the visitor through the pitch: three feature beats, each on its own painted scene that drifts with the pointer and the scroll; an honest trailer placeholder (a painted poster frame and a drawn play glyph, with a caption saying no video ships); a world-map teaser with glowing location chips naming invented places; then the release window — a season label only, never a date — above a row of drawn, deliberately generic store buttons; and the conversion close: an email capture that saves locally and says so, beside the wishlist buttons. The look is locked: deep violet-black dusk, one ember-orange accent, warm parchment text, Cinzel's inscription-cut display over Alegreya's bookish body, visible brushwork everywhere and no screenshots, no pixel art, no glass. Motion is slow weather: a four-second settle on the key art, parallax drift on the scenes, pulsing map dots — all with static fallbacks under prefers-reduced-motion and pointer parallax on fine pointers only. Every painting comes from one painter's dusk world with figures only as distant silhouettes and no readable text anywhere. Swap game, studio, world and release window in one content file; the second demo case turns the same page into a cozy lantern-keeping tale.

## Make it yours

- A solo developer launching one atmospheric game: set GAME and STUDIO to your project, WINDOW to your season, and rewrite the three feature beats around your own mechanics.
- A cozy game studio: use the second demo's lantern-keeping angle, set WORLD to your setting, and swap the map chips for your own invented locations.
- An agency building reveal pages: keep the painted hero and parallax scenes, change GAME, STUDIO and WINDOW per client, and point the store buttons at real storefronts.
- A narrative adventure team: replace the world-map chips with chapter names, keep the season-only WINDOW, and link the wishlist close to your mailing list.
