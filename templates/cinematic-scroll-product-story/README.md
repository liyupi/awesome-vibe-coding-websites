# Cinematic Scroll Product Story <sup>PRO</sup>

> A film-style scroll story for a hardware launch: pinned scenes, typed captions, counting specs and preorder.

This is a single-page launch site for a physical product that plays like a short film. The default story follows Arc 7, an electric commuter bike from an invented maker called Corvel, through six full-bleed scenes: a cold open on a ridge road at dusk, a wet city street, a range scene where a route draws itself across a contour map, a climb with a dot riding up an elevation profile, a night scene of streaking lights, and a final reveal that turns the bike through four angles before you reach the preorder panel. It suits hardware founders, direct-to-consumer brands and designers who need a launch or preorder page that makes people feel the product before they read the numbers. The pattern carries over to anything you can photograph from several angles, from scooters and audio gear to kitchen machines. The look is dark and cinematic: near-black scenes with warm dusk light, condensed uppercase headlines, film-style letterbox bars with a running timecode, and subtitles that type in one line at a time. One electric lime accent is reserved for the spec numbers, progress bars, price and reserve button, so the eye always lands on what matters. Each scene pins for about two screens of scrolling and crossfades into the next, with two or three parallax depth layers moving at different speeds. Range, torque, weight and charge time count up as their scene arrives. The preorder panel stays in view while you choose one of four colors, which recolor the bike image, pick a frame size and reserve with a refundable deposit; the choice is remembered in the browser and nothing is sent. To adapt it, change the brand, product name, product type, price and deposit; the prompt rewrites every caption, spec and FAQ answer to match.

## Make it yours

- An electric scooter brand: set [BRAND] and [PRODUCT] to your model, change [PRODUCT_TYPE] to electric scooter, and swap the six bike scenes for shots of the deck, stem and folded frame.
- An audio gear maker: keep the pinned scenes and typed captions, replace the bike images with your headphones from four angles, and rewrite the counting specs as battery life, weight and driver size.
- A kitchen appliance startup: set [PRODUCT_TYPE] to countertop oven, replace the ridge and city scenes with your product in a real kitchen, and turn the elevation profile into a temperature curve.
- A direct-to-consumer founder testing a launch page: set [PRICE] and [DEPOSIT] to your numbers, pick one electric lime accent for the reserve button, and link the preorder panel to your mailing list.

## FAQ

### How do I adapt the Cinematic Scroll Product Story to a product that isn't a bike?
The Cinematic Scroll Product Story template is built around the [PRODUCT_TYPE] variable, so change it to your product and the prompt rewrites every caption, spec and FAQ answer to match. The included bike images only fit a bicycle, so for scooters, audio gear or kitchen machines you replace the six scene images with your own shots from several angles and keep the pinned scenes, typed captions and counting specs.

### What do I need to change first to launch the Cinematic Scroll Product Story for my own brand?
Start with the five variables: set [BRAND] to your maker name, [PRODUCT] to your model name, [PRODUCT_TYPE] to what the product is, then [PRICE] and [DEPOSIT] to your full price and refundable reservation amount. The Cinematic Scroll Product Story template uses those values in the wordmark, timecode bar, reveal, preorder panel, confirmation and FAQ, so setting them first keeps the whole page consistent.

### Does the preorder form in the Cinematic Scroll Product Story send orders or take payments?
No. The preorder panel in the Cinematic Scroll Product Story lets a visitor pick one of four colors that recolor the bike image, choose a frame size and press reserve, but the choice is only remembered in the browser and nothing is sent anywhere. The template has no backend and no external APIs, so you connect the reserve button to your own checkout or mailing list before taking real deposits.

### How does the scrolling film effect work in the Cinematic Scroll Product Story?
The Cinematic Scroll Product Story pins each of its six full-bleed scenes for about two screens of scrolling, then crossfades into the next with two or three parallax depth layers moving at different speeds. Captions type in one line at a time, range, torque, weight and charge time count up as their scene arrives, and a reduced-motion setting turns the animation off for visitors who ask for it.
