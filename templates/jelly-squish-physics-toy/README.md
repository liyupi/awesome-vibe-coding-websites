Jelly Squish Physics Toy turns a small dessert into a tactile browser playground. A translucent, fluted jelly sits on a ceramic plate in a soft peach studio. It is ready to touch as soon as the page opens. Press into its surface, pull an edge, or let it fall onto the plate and watch the shape ripple back into place. The movement comes from a connected spring mesh, so the point you grab and the surrounding surface respond together.

The interface stays close to the object. A short invitation introduces the toy, three flavor chips change its color and character, and a stiffness slider moves the feel from loose wobble to a firmer bounce. A prominent Drop it button gives visitors a satisfying first interaction without needing to discover a gesture. Sound starts only after a deliberate action and can be muted. The little squelches are synthesized in the browser rather than loaded from recordings.

This prompt suits creative coding portfolios, playful campaign extras, dessert concepts, and makers exploring direct manipulation on the web. Its composition pairs large, friendly display type with a restrained control tray and plenty of room around the main object. Desktop visitors get a generous studio stage; phone visitors get the same visible toy and thumb-sized controls in a compact arrangement.

Change the fictional studio name, introductory copy, flavor collection, and accent color to make another edition. The shape, lighting, control hierarchy, and deformation behavior stay consistent while the content changes. All visual assets are created in code, fonts are bundled locally, and the result builds into static files. There is no account, checkout, or server to configure.

## FAQ

### What can visitors actually do on the Jelly Squish Physics Toy page?
Visitors poke, pull and drop a translucent fluted jelly sitting on a ceramic plate. Pressing the surface makes a local indentation, dragging an edge stretches it, and releasing produces a damped wobble. A Drop it button lifts the jelly about one jelly-height and lets it fall, compress and settle. Three flavor chips change the color, a stiffness slider moves the feel from loose to firm, and synthesized squelch sound starts only after a deliberate action.

### How do I change the jelly flavors and accent color in the Jelly Squish Physics Toy?
The Jelly Squish Physics Toy is controlled by the FLAVORS and ACCENT variables. FLAVORS takes exactly three flavor names with matching jelly color hex values, and ACCENT sets the primary button, active flavor outline, focus state and slider thumb. Set ACCENT to a sufficiently dark shade so labels and buttons stay readable, then rewrite the related flavor labels naturally so the copy matches the new names.

### How do I turn the Jelly Squish Physics Toy into a page for my own studio or brand?
Change BRAND to your studio name, HEADLINE to a short playful phrase, INTRO to your invitation line, and FLAVORS and ACCENT to your edition's colors. The shape, lighting, control hierarchy and deformation behavior stay as they are, so the page keeps working while the content changes. Keep the headline to five to seven words and the intro short, because the layout is built around a compact invitation beside the playable stage.

### Does the Jelly Squish Physics Toy need a server, account or external media to run?
No. The Jelly Squish Physics Toy builds into static files with no backend, no server code and no external APIs, and there is no account or checkout to configure. All visuals are created in code with Three.js, the squelch sounds are synthesized in the browser rather than loaded from recordings, and the fonts are bundled locally. You can host the built files on any static host.

### What should I check first after generating the Jelly Squish Physics Toy on a phone?
Check that the jelly and the Drop it control both appear on the first screen without a tall blank gap, since the mobile scene is 300 to 340px tall and the jelly should fill about 65 to 75 percent of the stage width. Confirm the flavor chips and stiffness slider have thumb-sized tap areas, and that dragging the jelly does not scroll the page while the rest of the page still scrolls normally.
