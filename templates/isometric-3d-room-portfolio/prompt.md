# Isometric 3D Room Portfolio

Build this website exactly as described below. Replace the [VARIABLES] with your own values; keep everything else.

## Brief
A single-page personal portfolio for a developer, staged as a cozy low-poly isometric 3D room. The room fills the screen; the bookshelf, desk monitor, guitar and wall mailbox are the navigation. Hover lifts and labels an object, click glides the camera to it and opens an About, Projects, Music or Contact panel, and a desk lamp flips the room between a pastel day and a deep blue night.

- Audience: Recruiters, studio leads and potential clients looking at [BRAND]'s work, plus other developers who will share a portfolio that is fun to poke at.
- Goal: Make [BRAND] memorable in under ten seconds, show four strong projects, and get the visitor to copy the email address or press 'Write an email'.
- Tone: Warm, playful and precise: a tidy toy diorama with honest, specific copy written in the first person. Short sentences, concrete numbers, no buzzwords.

### Layout & motion
- Spacing: Overlay inset 24px desktop / 16px mobile. Panel padding 28px desktop / 20px mobile, 20px gaps between panel blocks, 10–14px gaps inside lists. 4px base scale (4, 8, 10, 12, 14, 16, 20, 24, 28, 40).
- Radius: Overlay pills 999px; panels 24px; cards and images 18px; thumbnails 12px; 3D RoundedBox radius 0.06–0.12 world units so everything looks soft and toy-like.
- Motion: One shared state store drives everything: { focus: null | 'about' | 'projects' | 'music' | 'contact', hovered: string | null, night: boolean, playing: boolean, trackIndex: number }. Hovering an HTML nav item sets hovered to the same id as hovering the 3D object, so both highlight together.
- Motion: Easing: GSAP 'power3.inOut' for camera moves, 'power2.out' for hover lift, 'back.out(1.6)' for the intro drop-in. CSS transitions use cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1).
- Motion: Intro (once on load): the room group rises from y -1.2 to 0 and scales 0.92 to 1 over 1.2s power3.out; then the interactive objects (bookshelf, desk set, guitar, mailbox, lamp) drop from y +1.5 into place with a 70ms stagger, 0.6s back.out(1.6). The overlay chips fade in 300ms after.
- Motion: Idle life: the room group bobs y ±0.06 on a 6s sine; the plant's leaf group sways ±0.05 rad on a 3.4s sine; 40 dust motes (drei Sparkles, size 2.5, speed 0.25, opacity 0.5, color #FFFFFF by day and #FFC873 by night) drift inside the room volume; the monitor cursor block toggles visibility every 530ms; by day two code-drawn clouds slide across the window sky plane (loop 40s), by night 30 star points twinkle.
- Motion: Hover (pointer devices only): object group lifts y +0.15 over 0.25s power2.out, every mesh in it gets emissive #FFFFFF at intensity 0.16 (night: emissive #FFC873 at 0.22), cursor becomes pointer, and a drei <Html> label (pill, Fredoka 500 15px) appears 0.5 units above the object, fading and rising 6px over 180ms. The mailbox flag also rotates up 70 degrees; the guitar tilts 3 degrees.
- Motion: Focus: GSAP tweens a proxy { px, py, pz, tx, ty, tz, zoom } over 1.1s power3.inOut; every frame the orthographic camera is placed at proxy position, looks at the proxy target and applies proxy zoom with updateProjectionMatrix(). The panel slides in from the right (desktop: translateX(100%) to 0 over 450ms, starting 350ms into the camera move) or up from the bottom (mobile). Back/close or Escape reverses both, panel first, then camera home.
- Motion: Day/night: GSAP tweens a single nightMix value 0 to 1 (or back) over 0.9s power2.inOut; every frame each themed material lerps between its day and night color (THREE.Color.lerpColors), light intensities lerp between their day and night values, and the two backdrop gradient layers crossfade by opacity. The lamp head bulb's emissive intensity goes 0 to 2.5, the lamp point light 0 to 18, fairy lights 0 to 1.8. The UI switch thumb slides 24px and its icon swaps sun/moon with a 90-degree rotate.
- Motion: Music playing: the record disc in the Music panel spins (360 degrees per 2.4s, linear), the progress bar advances in real time, and in the room three small butter-colored music notes (code-drawn planes) float up from the amp and fade over 2.2s, looping with 0.7s offsets.
- Motion: prefers-reduced-motion: camera moves and the day/night change become instant (duration 0), no intro, no bob, no sway, no Sparkles, no floating notes, no record spin, panels appear without sliding (opacity only, 120ms). Hover keeps the emissive highlight and label but no lift.
- Imagery: The hero is the 3D room itself, a dense, polished diorama that fills the first screen, so there is no hero photo. Everything in the scene is modeled from code primitives (drei RoundedBox, boxes, cylinders, spheres, THREE.ExtrudeGeometry from 2D shapes) with flat meshStandardMaterial (roughness 0.85, metalness 0) and soft shadows. Wall art, the monitor screen and project thumbnails are code-drawn (CanvasTexture or inline SVG) as abstract shapes in the palette: no real posters, album covers, logos or readable brand marks anywhere. The only photographs are two AI-generated images of the fictional owner inside the About panel (a head-and-shoulders portrait and an over-the-shoulder desk shot), both in the same muted pastel peach-and-lavender grade as the room.

## Don'ts
- No backend, API routes, databases, auth or form submission; contact is copy-to-clipboard plus a mailto link only.
- No audio files, audio streaming or third-party embeds of any music or video service; the music player is a silent timer UI.
- No external 3D models, baked textures, HDRIs or Blender files: build every object from three.js primitives and code-drawn CanvasTextures; do not copy any published room portfolio's model, layout or furniture arrangement.
- No character avatars, mascots, pets as 3D figures or drivable vehicles in the scene.
- No real brand logos or recognizable product designs on any object: plain monitor, plain keyboard, generic guitar headstock, no consoles, sneakers or branded gear.
- No real posters, album covers, game or anime artwork; wall art is code-drawn abstract shapes only.
- No real artist names, real song titles or copyrighted music anywhere; all tracks and the alias are invented.
- The portrait and desk photo show a fictional AI-generated person only, never a real person's likeness.
- No copy, slogans or section names lifted from any existing portfolio; write all copy fresh in US English.
- Self-host everything: fonts through Fontsource npm packages, icons through lucide-react, images downloaded into public/images/. No CDN <script> or <link> tags, no Google Fonts, no analytics.
- No commercial fonts and no restricted UI library source (Aceternity UI, Tailwind Plus / Tailwind UI); write the overlay components yourself.
- Production build with one command (npm run build) to dist/: minified, no source maps, relative asset paths (Vite base './'), no src/ copied into dist/, dist/ under 10 MB.
- Single page: every internal link is ./index.html or a hash route (#/about, #/projects, #/music, #/contact); never link to a bare directory.
- No page scroll on the stage; only panel content scrolls. No OrbitControls or free camera; the camera only moves through the focus tweens.
- Must work at 1440×900 and 390×844 with zero console errors; every section is reachable by keyboard through the HTML nav, Escape closes panels, focus rings are visible.
- Respect prefers-reduced-motion: instant camera cuts and day/night switch, no intro, bob, sway, particles, notes or spinning record.
- Wrap every localStorage read and write (night mode, last track) in try/catch; the site must work with storage blocked.
- Never mention VibeFather or any other brand inside the site except the optional credit footer link; no dates or years in the copy.
