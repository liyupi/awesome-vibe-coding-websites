# Physics Playground Landing Page

Build this website exactly as described below. Replace the [VARIABLES] with your own values; keep everything else.

## Brief
A one-page landing site for [BRAND], [PRODUCT]. The first screen is a toy: inside a framed canvas-style arena, every letter of [HERO_WORD] and nine small UI chips (a Share button, a toggle, swatches, live-cursor tags, a slider) are separate rigid bodies driven by a hand-rolled 2D physics engine. They drop in staggered from above, bounce, stack and settle on the arena floor; visitors can grab any piece with mouse or touch and throw it, and on phones tilting the device redirects gravity. Below the hero the page calms down into a conventional SaaS rhythm: a feature marquee, a six-card feature grid where each card holds a small looping physics vignette, a three-tier pricing table with a monthly/yearly toggle, testimonials with portraits, a sign-up band that rains confetti blocks after a local-only submit, an FAQ and a footer.

- Audience: [AUDIENCE] who want a tool that feels playful but produces exact, hand-off-ready work; secondarily, developers and indie founders who want to learn how a from-scratch 2D physics loop works and reuse it on their own product page.
- Goal: Make visitors stay and play on the first screen, then move them down the page to pick a plan and leave an email in the sign-up band.
- Tone: Warm, witty and concrete. Short sentences, real numbers, no buzzwords. The visuals are tactile and sticker-like: chunky rounded blocks, thick near-black outlines, hard offset shadows, candy colors on a warm off-white canvas, bouncy but never chaotic motion.

  - Physics module in src/physics/ (pure TypeScript, no DOM except in render.ts): vec.ts (tiny 2D vector helpers: add, sub, scale, dot, cross, rotate, length), body.ts (Body type: id, w, h, x, y (center), angle, vx, vy, av, invMass, invInertia, restitution, friction, isStatic, sleeping, sleepTime, grabbed), collide.ts (OBB vs OBB with the separating axis theorem over the 4 face normals, returning normal, penetration and 1–2 contact points found by clipping the incident edge against the reference face), world.ts (World class: bodies, gravity {x,y}, step(dt), addBody, removeBody, wakeAll, setBounds(width,height)), drag.ts (pointer grab and throw), loop.ts (fixed-timestep runner), render.ts (writes each body's transform to its DOM element).
  - Engine constants (in src/physics/world.ts, exported so readers can tweak them): fixed step 1/120 s; at most 5 substeps per animation frame (drop leftover time beyond that); gravity magnitude G = 2600 px/s^2 pointing down; density 0.001 so mass = w*h*0.001 and inertia = mass*(w*w + h*h)/12; linear damping 0.02 per second and angular damping 0.6 per second applied as v *= 1 / (1 + damping*dt); max linear speed 4000 px/s; restitution 0.3 for letter blocks and 0.45 for chips; friction 0.55 for everything; static walls have invMass 0 and invInertia 0.
  - World.step(dt): (1) integrate velocities of awake, non-static bodies with gravity and damping; (2) broad phase: skip pairs whose axis-aligned bounding boxes do not overlap and pairs where both bodies are asleep or static; (3) narrow phase with collide.ts; wake a sleeping body when an awake body touches it with relative speed above 30 px/s; (4) sequential-impulse solver, 10 iterations, per contact point: normal impulse with restitution applied only when approach speed exceeds 60 px/s, accumulated normal impulse clamped to >= 0, Coulomb friction impulse clamped to +/- friction * normal impulse, using the standard effective mass 1/(invMassA + invMassB + (rA x n)^2 invIA + (rB x n)^2 invIB); (5) integrate positions and angles; (6) positional correction: push each pair apart along the normal by max(penetration - 0.5, 0) * 0.35, split by inverse mass; (7) sleep: a body whose speed < 8 px/s and |av| < 0.08 rad/s for 0.6 s goes to sleep (velocities zeroed); a grabbed body never sleeps.
  - Bounds: each world has three static walls built from its container size: a floor whose top edge is the container bottom (200 px thick), and left and right walls outside the container edges (200 px thick, 4000 px tall, extending far above the top). There is no ceiling: thrown pieces fly up and fall back. If any body ends up more than 120 px outside the container (after a resize, for instance) teleport it to just above the floor at the nearest valid x with zero velocity.
  - loop.ts: Runner class with start(), stop(), and an accumulator over requestAnimationFrame timestamps (clamp frame delta to 1/20 s). It stops itself when every body is asleep and nothing is grabbed, and restarts on wake (pointer down, tilt change, Reset, Shake, resize). Each world's container is watched by an IntersectionObserver: out of view means stop, back in view means start. document visibilitychange also stops and starts it.
  - render.ts: each body maps to an absolutely positioned element (top 0, left 0, will-change transform) inside its container. Every frame set transform = translate3d(x - w/2 px, y - h/2 px, 0) rotate(angle rad). Keep the hard shadow pointing down-right whatever the rotation: compute the offset (6, 6) rotated by -angle, round to 0.5 px, and set style.boxShadow = `${sx}px ${sy}px 0 #1A1714` only when the angle changed by more than 0.01 rad since the last write.
  - drag.ts: pointerdown on a body element calls setPointerCapture, marks the body grabbed and awake, stores the grab point in body-local coordinates and adds class is-grabbed (inner wrapper scales to 1.06, shadow offset grows from 6 to 10 px, z-index 50, cursor grabbing). Each step, a spring-damper at the grab point pulls the body toward the pointer: desired velocity = (pointer - grabWorldPoint) * 25, clamped to 3500 px/s, applied as an impulse at the grab point equal to (desired - velocityAtGrabPoint) * effectiveMass * 0.5, so bodies swing and rotate naturally when held off-center. Keep the last 6 pointer samples with timestamps; on pointerup or pointercancel, set the body's linear velocity to the average pointer velocity over the last 100 ms (clamped to 3000 px/s), keep its angular velocity, and release. Hovered bodies show cursor grab. Set touch-action: none on every body element and on the arena so dragging never scrolls the page, but leave the page scrollable by touching the empty arena background (touch-action pan-y on the arena background layer).
  - src/hero/arena.ts builds the hero world from content.ts: one letter body per non-space character of [HERO_WORD] and one body per chip, creates their DOM, places spawn positions, runs the drop-in, and wires Reset, Shake and tilt. src/hero/tilt.ts handles DeviceOrientationEvent. src/features/vignettes.ts creates one small World per feature card. src/signup/confetti.ts creates the sign-up band's confetti world. src/sections/*.ts each export a function returning that section's HTML string from content.ts; main.ts concatenates them into #app, then mounts the interactive parts (header, arena, marquee, vignettes, pricing toggle, FAQ, sign-up).
  - Before the first build, download every image listed under Assets into public/images/ with curl -L -o using the exact file names given. The build itself never fetches anything.
  - public/favicon.svg as described under Assets. index.html: lang en, <meta name='theme-color' content='#FBF6EC'>, <link rel='icon' href='./favicon.svg' type='image/svg+xml'>, <title>[BRAND] — sketch interface ideas together</title> (rewrite the part after the dash to fit [PRODUCT] when it changes).
  - Write ASSETS.md at the project root as described under Assets. dist/ holds index.html, hashed JS/CSS, the woff2 font files, favicon.svg and the three portraits; keep it under 5 MB.

## Variables
- [BRAND] — Brand name (default: "Wobblebox"). Invented product name, one or two words. Used in the wordmark, title, footer blocks, email (hello@ + emailDomain) and localStorage keys. Never a real company.
- [PRODUCT] — What the product is (default: "a browser canvas for sketching interface ideas together"). One lowercase phrase starting with 'a' or 'an'. Drives the hero subline, title, feature cards, chip labels and FAQ. Works best for a playful creative or productivity tool.
- [AUDIENCE] — Who it is for (default: "product designers and the engineers who build their screens"). One lowercase phrase. Used in the hero note, pricing intro and testimonials roles.
- [HERO_WORD] — Physics headline (default: "SKETCH LOOSE"). The letters that fall in the hero. 6–14 letters, one or two words, uppercase. Each letter becomes a draggable block; a space separates the words.
- [CREDIT] — keep a small "Template by VibeFather" link to https://vibefather.ai/t/physics-playground-landing-page in the footer (default: yes; delete this line to remove it)

The copy under Pages & Sections is written for the default values. If your values differ, rewrite every headline, paragraph, label and image for them — same length, tone and structure. Layout, colors, type and motion stay as described.
Keep all copy, image paths and links in one content file (for example `src/content.ts`), keyed by section, so the content can be swapped later without touching components.

- Motion: Hero drop-in: letters spawn in reading order, 90 ms apart, from y = -140 to -620 px above the arena top with a random angle of +/-0.15 rad, then chips spawn 70 ms apart after the last letter at random x positions; all falling, bouncing and settling comes from the physics engine, not CSS.
- Motion: Buttons press like stickers: hover translate(-2px,-2px) with shadow 4px to 6px over 120ms ease-out; active translate(2px,2px) with shadow 0 over 60ms.
- Motion: Grabbed bodies lift: inner wrapper scale 1.06 and shadow offset 10px while held, spring back to 1.0 and 6px over 180ms cubic-bezier(.34,1.56,.64,1) on release.
- Motion: Marquee band scrolls left continuously at 40 s per loop, pauses on hover.
- Motion: Section headings and cards enter with a 'pop' when 20% visible: from translateY(24px) scale(0.96) opacity 0 to rest over 520ms cubic-bezier(.34,1.56,.64,1), cards staggered 70ms; triggered once by IntersectionObserver.
- Motion: Feature vignettes drop their pieces when the card enters view, then loop: after all pieces sleep and 5 s pass without interaction, pieces shrink to scale 0 over 200ms and re-drop.
- Motion: Pricing toggle knob slides with a 260ms overshoot easing; price digits roll vertically (old value slides up and out, new value slides up from below) over 250ms.
- Motion: Testimonial cards rest at small rotations (-2deg, 1.5deg, -1deg) and straighten to 0deg with translateY(-4px) on hover over 200ms.
- Motion: prefers-reduced-motion: no drop-in, no tilt, no Shake, no idle loops, no marquee movement, no pop-ins, no digit rolls. Hero and vignette pieces render pre-settled in a composed layout; dragging still works and released pieces settle under normal gravity.
- Imagery: The hero's visual anchor is the framed physics arena itself: a polished, invented canvas-app mock (dotted grid, mini toolbar, candy letter blocks and UI chips with thick outlines and hard shadows), never typography on a flat background. All UI chips, vignette pieces, icons and decorative shapes are drawn in code with HTML/CSS and Lucide icons; no screenshots of any real design tool and no product chrome that resembles one. The only raster images are three AI-generated testimonial avatars of fictional people, drawn as one series: flat vector illustration, no outlines, no facial features, warm off-white background with a soft pastel blob, head and shoulders. Portraits are cropped to 64px circles with a 3px ink border.

## Don'ts
- No physics library of any kind (Matter.js, Planck.js, Rapier, Box2D ports, p2, cannon): the 2D engine is written from scratch in src/physics/.
- No backend: no server code, API routes, databases, auth or third-party APIs. The sign-up form only writes to localStorage and never sends data anywhere.
- No real design-tool brand names, logos, UI screenshots or recognizable product chrome. Every chip, toolbar and mock UI piece is generic and invented under [BRAND].
- No copy, slogans or headline wording taken from existing physics-hero sites or design-tool marketing pages; write all copy fresh from this prompt.
- No mascots, characters or borrowed illustrations; decorative shapes are generic geometry drawn in code.
- No real pricing, user counts or customer logos; all figures, testimonials, names and companies are invented. No logo wall.
- Fonts are self-hosted through Fontsource only. Never load fonts from fonts.googleapis.com, fonts.gstatic.com or any other CDN, and use no commercial fonts.
- No CDN <script> or <link> tags, no analytics, no hotlinked images: every image is downloaded into public/images/ before building.
- No Unsplash, Pexels, Pixabay or other stock-site images, and no placeholder-image services.
- Production build only: minified, no source maps, no src/ copied into dist/, relative asset paths (base './'), dist/ under 20 MB.
- Every internal link points to a file name (./index.html or ./index.html#section), never to a bare directory or '/'.
- No mention of VibeFather anywhere in the site except an optional credit link in the footer.
- Do not let dragging scroll the page or cause console errors; the site must run at 1440x900 and 390x844 with zero console errors and no horizontal scroll.
- Do not run the physics loop when the arena or a vignette is off-screen, when the tab is hidden, or when every body is asleep.
- Respect prefers-reduced-motion: no drop-in, tilt, Shake, idle loops, marquee movement or pop-ins; render composed, pre-settled layouts with drag still working.
- Do not request device-orientation permission on page load; only from a tap on the Enable tilt pill, and never show that pill where permission is not required.
- Do not change the stack (Vite 5, vanilla TypeScript, Tailwind CSS 3.4) or add UI frameworks unless the user asks.
