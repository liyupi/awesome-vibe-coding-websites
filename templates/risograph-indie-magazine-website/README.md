# Risograph Indie Magazine Website

> A fluorescent print magazine with overprinted cover art, full stories, and a local print subscription demo.

A magazine should feel as considered as the object a reader carries home. This website opens with a large, code-drawn issue cover, fluorescent pink and process blue plates, visible halftones, and a small yellow paper tab. The colors overlap into purple while the reading surfaces stay clean and legible. A compact masthead, oversized issue theme, and practical reading links give the opening screen the rhythm of a culture publication rather than a product launch.

The default publication explores the small spaces where people make things together. Its story grid includes a shared repair bench, a listening room, neighborhood printmaking, and the quiet logistics of a communal dinner. Readers can filter by section, save a story locally, and open a complete long read with original paragraphs, a pull quote, an illustration, and a reading progress line. Contributor cards use original geometric portraits and specific editorial roles. Every article has content rather than a dead read-more link.

A print section compares a single issue with a small subscription bundle and shows the arithmetic before a local confirmation. It does not collect payment or claim to send an email. The second example changes the publication into a design-and-making journal, with different headlines, contributors, article prose, and print pricing while keeping the same visual system.

All artwork is made in SVG and CSS, so there are no external photographs to license or replace. Content lives in one module, fonts are bundled locally, and reduced-motion mode removes the plate drift. The layout works as a useful starting point for independent magazines, cultural journals, and small editorial collectives with something substantial to say.

## Make it yours

- A neighborhood arts journal: set BRAND to your journal, CITY to your district, and rewrite the six stories around local studios, workshops and shared tools.
- A design-and-making zine: keep the visual system, change ISSUE_THEME to a materials theme, and rewrite contributor roles to makers, illustrators and printmakers.
- A small press selling issues: set PRINT_PRICE to your cover price, describe your paper and binding in the print desk copy, and link the confirmation to your own order form.
- A cultural collective archive: keep the filter sections Places, People and Objects as your catalog, and swap the saved stories for a reading list of past issues.

## FAQ

### How do I change the magazine name, city and issue theme in the Risograph Indie Magazine Website?
The Risograph Indie Magazine Website template exposes four variables: BRAND, ISSUE_THEME, CITY and PRINT_PRICE. Set BRAND to the publication name, ISSUE_THEME to the cover line, CITY to where it is based and PRINT_PRICE to the cover price. The brief asks the agent to rewrite every headline, standfirst, contributor note, alt text and confirmation so nothing still mentions the default Sidefold, Port Briar or Small Acts, Loud Places.

### Can I add real photographs to the Risograph Indie Magazine Website instead of the SVG artwork?
The Risograph Indie Magazine Website template ships with original SVG and CSS artwork only: halftone tables, ribbed benches, circular speakers, print rollers and geometric contributor portraits. There are no external photographs to license or replace. If you want photos, ask your AI agent to swap the motif for an image while keeping the pink and process blue ink treatment and the purple overprint, and keep text off the grain layer so labels stay readable.

### Does the print subscription on the Risograph Indie Magazine Website take payment or send emails?
No. The print desk on the Risograph Indie Magazine Website is a local demo. You choose single issue or three-issue bundle, a quantity from 1 to 4, an email and a country, and the form shows a live total and an in-place receipt that says nothing was charged, ordered or emailed. Choosing Other for country disables the reservation because international shipping is not simulated. Treat it as a layout to connect to your own ordering flow later.

### How do readers save stories, and where does the saved list live on the Risograph Indie Magazine Website?
Each story card on the Risograph Indie Magazine Website has a separate Save story action beside Read story. Saved IDs are kept in the browser's local storage under a namespaced key, so they survive a reload but stay on that device. The masthead bookmark button shows the count, and the story desk has a Saved toggle that combines with the All, Places, People and Objects filters, with an empty state when nothing matches.

### What should I edit first in the Risograph Indie Magazine Website to make it my own publication?
Start with the single content module, which holds every headline, standfirst, article paragraph, contributor note and print price. Rewrite the issue theme and cover intro first, then the six stories with your own subjects and read times, then the four contributor roles and portraits. Keep the two-ink palette, the halftone artwork and the reading progress line, since those carry the risograph feel.
